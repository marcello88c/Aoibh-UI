# Competitor Comparison Content — Parked

Status: **Not now. Revisit once real client results exist.**

## The idea

A comparison chart/page positioning Aoibh against Design Pickle and
Superside (the two names that came up as reference points during this
build).

## Why it's parked, not dropped

Raised and discussed directly — the conclusion was that it's premature for
reasons beyond just "not enough traffic yet":

- **No proof points yet.** A comparison chart implicitly claims "we're
  proven better." Right now there's no live traffic, no real clients, no
  case studies — the pricing itself is still derived from margin math, not
  validated by real usage. Making a bold comparative claim next to that is
  a credibility mismatch.
- **Accuracy is a real legal exposure, not just a marketing risk.**
  Comparative advertising naming specific competitors is generally legal,
  but only if claims are truthful, current, and not misleading. Their
  pricing/features will drift over time — this can't be written once and
  left stale if it's ever built.
- **Reads as reactive, not confident.** A brand-new studio's first public
  move being "look how we compare to the established players" cuts against
  the premium positioning the rest of the site is going for.

## What to do instead, for now

Keep describing what makes Aoibh different in its own terms — 80% match
confidence, the 2-day journey tracker, the AI + human re-check — without
naming competitors. That doesn't require anyone to verify a claim against a
competitor to trust it.

## When this becomes worth building

Once there's real usage data or actual client results to back specific
claims. At that point, the right format is a dedicated **comparison page in
the Journal** (e.g. "Aoibh vs. Design Pickle vs. Superside") rather than a
homepage chart — this is a legitimate, common B2B tactic (see the SEO
conversation notes: comparison content captures people already comparing
options, which is high-intent search traffic) but it needs to be a
considered, carefully-researched page built on something real, not written
speculatively.
