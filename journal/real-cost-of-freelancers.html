The real cost of freelancers isn't the invoice

Aoibh Journal6 min read

A freelancer's day rate is the number everyone compares. It's also the smallest part of what a freelance hire actually costs. The real expense shows up as your own time — finding them, vetting them, chasing them, and re-explaining the brief they've already forgotten — and almost nobody tracks that number, which is exactly why it never factors into the decision.

Where the time actually goes

Finding someone

Posting the job, scanning portfolios, messaging five people to hear back from two — before any actual design work starts, there's a real search cost. Multiply that by however many times a year a new project needs a designer you haven't worked with before.

Vetting them

A polished portfolio tells you what someone is capable of at their absolute best, on their own schedule, with no client pressure. It tells you almost nothing about how they perform on a tight deadline, or how they respond when you ask for a change they don't agree with — and there's usually no way to find that out except by hiring them and finding out the hard way.

Re-briefing, every time

Every new freelancer starts from zero. They don't know your brand history, your past approved work, or the reasoning behind decisions made six months ago — all of which has to be re-explained, every single time, because there's no continuity between hires.

Chasing

Following up on a draft that's late. Re-explaining a note that got misread. Wondering whether radio silence means "still working on it" or "took another job and went quiet." None of this is a design cost. It's a management cost, and it falls entirely on you.

Quality roulette

Even with a great freelancer, there's no guarantee the next one is anywhere close — every new hire resets the quality bar back to unknown, with no accumulated track record to lean on.

None of this shows up on an invoice. All of it shows up as your own time, which is the actual scarce resource in most small teams.

What this actually costs, roughly

Say finding, vetting, and managing a single freelance project realistically costs 3-4 hours of your own time beyond the design work itself — sourcing, briefing, chasing, reviewing. At any reasonable value for a founder or marketer's time, that's a real, recurring cost that never appears in a budget line anywhere, because nobody's tracking "hours spent managing the freelancer" as its own expense.

Why this is a process problem, not a talent problem

The issue usually isn't finding good individual freelancers — plenty exist. It's that each one is a fresh, unproven relationship, and the overhead of managing that uncertainty repeats every single time, regardless of how good any one person turns out to be.

A vetted network removes the search and vetting cost, since the screening already happened once, not per-project. A single ongoing point of contact removes the re-briefing cost, since context carries over between projects instead of resetting to zero each time. And a defined process — matching, drafting, review, delivery — removes the chasing, because status is visible instead of something you have to ask about.
